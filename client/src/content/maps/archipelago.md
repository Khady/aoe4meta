## Map Overview

Pure water map where **naval supremacy decides outcomes**. Island hopping is necessary for expansion, and trade ships become vulnerable targets.

- Full aquatic: there is **no land bridge** between players. Transport ships are the only way to land armies.
- Each player starts on their own safe-ish home island; if you control the water, you can **block enemy transports** and stay secure.
- Several **neutral side/mid islands** exist. These matter a ton later for extra wood and map control.
- Small patches of **water stealth (reeds/kelp)** appear in a few spots; ships inside aren't visible unless revealed—great for ambushes.

## Key Resources

- Island-based resources must be secured
- Abundant fish provides main food source
- Naval trade routes are lucrative but risky
- **Food on land (home island):** typically light — 1 berry patch, a few sheep, **1 deer herd**, **1 boar**. Plan to lean on fishing.
- **Fish types:** deep-water fish **regenerate** over time (boats only); **shore fish do not regenerate** and can be gathered by villagers.
- **Gold (home island):** **2 × 8000** deposits + **1 × 4000** deposit — plenty to tech up if you keep water eco running.
- **Stone (home island):** about **2400** starting stone. Mostly for a few outposts/keeps on shorelines or islands.
- **Wood:** looks plentiful but **drains very fast** on this map because ships eat wood. Expect to **expand to side islands** for lumber midgame.
- **Sacred Sites & Relics:** usually **2 Sacred Sites** on outer islands plus scattered relics; contestable only via transports.
- **Coastal Trade Posts:** present on coasts—enable **water trade** once you have naval control (yields **gold + wood** per trip for trade ships).

## Strategic Approach

Prioritize naval units immediately - without ships you cannot compete. Secure multiple islands to ensure resource access and map control.

Protect your trade routes while raiding enemy trade. The civilization that controls the seas controls the game.

### Early Game (I → II)
- **Open Dock + Fishing** fast; balance eco so villagers gather **wood/gold/stone** while **boats handle food**.
- Scout just needs to **reveal your island**. After that it has low value; you can park it on a likely landing beach as an early **transport spotter**.
- Start contesting water ASAP; **losing the water** = losing fishing **and** giving opponent safe trade.

### Mid Game (II → III)
- **Wood management is everything.** As main forests thin, **pre-emptively settle a side island** with a transport and a few vills.
- Build **outposts/keeps** on key shores/islands to shut down enemy naval passage and punish landings.
- Use **transport plays**:
  - **Forward production** on enemy coast (risky but disruptive), or
  - Quick **raids/drop-offs**—always escort transports with navy.
- Grab **relics & Sacred Sites** using **monks in transports** once you have naval presence.

### Ambush & Control Tricks
- **Stealth water patches** are perfect for **Demolition Ship** traps. Hide a few; detonate into clumped enemy fleets.
- Shoreline towers placed to overlap can **melt ships** trying to rush past, forcing the opponent to bring late-age warships or siege options.

### Teching & Power Spikes
- Don't be shy about **aging up**; double economy (boats + villagers) lets you hit upgrades quickly.
- **Castle Age naval techs** (names vary by civ) are **must-haves**: e.g., hull/armor upgrades (~**+20% ship HP** and **+1 ranged armor**), and **springald-ship buffs** (often **+1 range** and **~+20% attack speed**). These swing equal-number fights.
- **Imperial Age spike:** unlock **Age IV warships** and a crucial **springald-ship upgrade** that adds **an extra 360° cannon**—mass springald fleets scale absurdly well with this.

### Trade on Water (when ahead)
- With water control, set **long trade routes** between your Dock(s) and **coastal trade posts**. Naval traders yield **gold + wood** (unlike land traders who give only gold), supercharging both teching and ship spam. Guard the line with a few warships.

## Recommended Civilizations (water, no land link)

- **Templier** – Top pick. Stellar wood economy (no lumber camps; instant drop-off), **~15% faster wood**, no walking time, and **~20% of wood gathered converted to food**. Also has **Pilgrimage** mechanics: pilgrims can reach Sacred Sites by boat for extra gold. Perfectly suited to ship-heavy play.
- **Chinese / Zhu Xi's Legacy** – Excellent eco scaling. **Imperial Officers** boost lumber camps (faster techs like Double Broadaxe/Forestry, more drop-off efficiency) and **tax gold** fuels upgrades. Gardens/Academy variants add passive resources or tax multipliers—great for sustained fleets.
- **Holy Roman Empire** – **Prelates** grant up to **+35% gather rate** aura; **Aachen** coverage on woodlines turbo-charges ship production.
- **Rus-like "dragon"** variant – Similar "chapel" style eco aura but a bit weaker; still solid for wood throughput.
- **Japanese** – **Cheaper fishing ships** helps early, but shines more on hybrid/landed maps with **Shinobi** land pressure; on Archipelago they're good, not S-tier.
- **English** – Lacks raw eco bonuses, but **Admiralty** tech grants **+1 range** to combat ships. It's pricey (you'll be behind briefly), but once researched you can **kite** and take favorable fights.

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] Open Dock + fishing; keep villager eco on wood/gold/stone.
- [ ] Contest water immediately; never let wood float—turn it into ships.
- [ ] Expand to a **side island for wood** before your main forests run dry.
- [ ] Tower/keep key crossings and beaches.
- [ ] Secure **relics & Sacred Sites** with transport micro.
- [ ] Hit **Castle** for core naval techs; mass **springald ships** and scale into **Imperial** for the 360° cannon upgrade.
- [ ] When ahead, **start water trade** (gold + wood) and snowball.
